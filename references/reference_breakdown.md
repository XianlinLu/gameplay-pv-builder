## What to preserve

- The first mechanic appears early.
- Selection UI establishes rules before combat.
- Traversal connects story and combat instead of functioning as filler.
- Combat escalates from ordinary enemies to a stronger named opponent.
- The results screen closes the loop and makes the sequence feel playable.

## What to redesign

Do not reuse the reference title, logo, character identity, Japanese setting, UI artwork, enemy designs, or on-screen copy by default. Translate the pacing and information architecture into the user's own game world.

## Timing adaptation

For a 45-second master, a useful starting allocation is:

- 0–3 seconds: hook/title;
- 3–10 seconds: character or loadout;
- 10–16 seconds: objective and first control;
- 16–23 seconds: traversal or discovery;
- 23–35 seconds: primary combat loop;
- 35–42 seconds: boss or climax;
- 42–45 seconds: result/end card.

Treat these as editorial targets, not fixed shot lengths. Extend a mechanic when comprehension requires it and compensate elsewhere.
